## Ground Rules

The only rule that is absolutely necessary is that I am not allowed to use any moves that do direct damage. Struggle is also included. Besides that, I am implementing four extra rules:

1. I cannot use any Legendary Pokémon.
2. I cannot use any items in Trainer Battles. (Held items are okay.)
    - This rule is intended to stop me from wasting the opponent's PP while not wasting mine *and* healing myself in the process.
3. The battle style is adjusted to "SET". This means that after I defeat a Pokémon, I can't switch Pokémon immediately.
4. I cannot trade with other players. (Trades with NPCs are fine.)
    - Shyguymask, who did a similar challenge in Pokémon HeartGold, also didn't trade Pokémon with other players, and thus never got a Gengar. However, they never stated that rule explicitly. They had a Steelix for the battle with Red, but that's because wild Steelix do exist in Pokémon HeartGold and SoulSilver.

## Before defeating Brock

### Starter Selection

The obvious choice is **Bulbasaur**, as it gets a multitude of useful moves early. It gets Leech Seed at level 7, and later Poison Powder and Sleep Powder at level 15. It also gets Synthesis at level 53 as Venusaur (Bulbasaur: 39, Ivysaur: 47).

Charmander and Squirtle don't really get as useful moves.

### First Catch

Your first catch should be **Caterpie**, which comes with String Shot. Raise it to level 7 to evolve it into Metapod, which will learn Harden when it evolves. I specifically call for catching Caterpie instead of Metapod for that access to String Shot, so you won't run out of PP as quickly.

### The Grind

You have to continuously run into Metapod or Kakuna in Viridian Forest. Their only move is Harden, so once they run out of PP for that move, they'll start using Struggle. Note that this is Gen 3, where Struggle's recoil depends on how much damage is actually dealt, and thus it can take a long time. Make sure Bulbasaur also uses Growl to lower Struggle's damage output. You need Potions, as Struggle will deal a minimum of 2 damage even at -6 Attack, and even more if that ends up being a critical hit.

You have to continue like this until level 7, when Bulbasaur learns Leech Seed. You will have to go to the Pokémon Center many times.

### Viridian Forest and Pewter Gym

Leech Seed is probably your only tool to get through Viridian Forest. Switch a lot between Bulbasaur and Caterpie to waste less PP. Caterpie will eventually evolve into Metapod, and you'd want it to stay as a Metapod when you get to Brock.

Brock is really easy. All you have to do is use Leech Seed on both of his Pokémon and wait. Onix may have Rock Tomb, but the Generation III AI is coded to treat Rock Tomb as a move stricly for speed control. Meaning that Onix will never use Rock Tomb on a slower Pokémon unless that results in a KO. Metapod will know Harden, so do use that.

## Before defeating Misty

### Route 3, Mt. Moon, and Route 4

While in Mt. Moon, you want to catch a **Zubat**. It learns Supersonic at level 11, and Confuse Ray at level 28 as a Golbat (Zubat gets it at 26). Golbat additionally gets Screech from the Pokémon Move Maniac on Two Island. Screech is especially useful because it increases confusion damage.

Supersonic will be necessary to defeat a Lass's Oddish and Bellsprout, plus those same Pokémon later down the road. Both are immune to both poisoning and Leech Seed. Bellsprout is especially annoying because it knows Wrap. There is a single Oran Berry at Route 3, and more are held by wild Jigglypuff.

At this stage in the game, you will likely have Metapod evolved into Butterfree, and Bulbasaur into Ivysaur. The latter will also have learned Poison Powder and Sleep Powder.

While on Route 4, you'll need to catch **Ekans**. Two of them, each with a different Ability. One with Intimidate, the other with Shed Skin. You can tell those apart when the encounter begins. Both would also know Leer, which at this point is the move to use before you get access to Screech.

### Cerulean City, Routes 24 and 25

You'd need the Ekans with Intimidate for the battle with your rival. His Pidgeotto is surprisingly strong for this point in the game, as it's evolved. And be careful, it knows Sand Attack. Abra knows no attacks, so use anything you like. (Teleport has no effect in Trainer Battles until LGPE.)

### Cerulean Gym

Misty is not super difficult or anything. The only thing you need to know is that her Starmie knows Rapid Spin, which removes Leech Seed if it hits successfully. We can't get a spinblocker at this point.

Well, I underestimated her. Staryu and Starmie both know Recover. While this may sound semi-good, since it has 5 PP in *Pokémon Scarlet and Violet*, this move has 20 PP in Gen 3. Bad news.

Staryu was really annoying, since it would use Recover a lot. Misty also used her one Super Potion on it. Starmie wasn't as annoying, but that's by accident. I meant to use Poison Powder, but used Sleep Powder instead. Starmie also never actually used Rapid Spin. It preferred raw power, using Swift and Water Pulse.

I guess the Gen 3 AI is not that smart after all...

## Before defeating Lt. Surge

### Vermilion Gym

Lt. Surge seems easy. All of his Pokémon are vulnerable to both Leech Seed and poisoning. However, Pikachu and Raichu both know Double Team, so it may be difficult to land moves. Both also know Thunder Wave.

You can get a Cheri Berry on the S.S. Anne. You *could* get more via Pickup, but because battles take longer in this challenge, it is not really worthwhile. The only Pokémon with Pickup, Meowth, is not otherwise useful in these conditions.
