I decided to buy a mechanical keyboard. This entire blog post was written using one. But let's first look at why I decided to get one in the first place.

## The reason I bought a keyboard

Last Thursday, I took apart my gaming laptop, which is an Acer Nitro 5. I wanted to remove the keyboard, so I can remove the gunk and hair collected under it over the 5 years of me owning it. I *should* have known that this is not very feasible because the keyboard is riveted into the palmrest, instead of simply being screwed into it.

So I did something risky, and basically tried to pry the spacebar out of the keyboard. I knew there was going to be some gunk in there. In the process, it appears that I broke some delicate plastic and now the spacebar isn't registering presses properly. I would now need to press it closer to the center.

## The keyboard itself

The keyboard is a Ziyoulang K96. (The brand has since renamed to Free Wolf, and as I can see based on the QC sticker on the back, it passed QC in March 2024.) It has red switches for all keys, which are arranged into a 100-key layout. Certain keycaps are colored dark gray or orange, and the rest are white. The keyboard comes with a physical on/off switch. It is wireless, so it can connect to an included USB receiver over 2.4 GHz, or via Bluetooth.

The seller says that the keyboard had only been in use for 2 months before listing it. He was also kind enough to provide two AAA batteries that power it up.
